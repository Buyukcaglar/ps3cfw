## Native fill and checkpoints

The old 16 x 98 GiB empty-PNY proposal is superseded. Compute the payload budget
from Lexar's actual post-population native available-byte output. Use only fresh
nonce-owned ordinary test files; retain game files, saves and the original image.
The current AW binary caps payload at 33 MiB and cannot perform a long fill.

Before release, implement and review the adaptive writer, ownership receipts,
distinct file/offset patterns, full readback, cross-file alias detection, progress,
and a demonstrated operator cancellation route. The draft AX control-file route
is not live cancellation qualification and must not be executed unsealed.

First target: stop with at least 128 GiB available, plus metadata/next-write
headroom. Verify every generated byte and preselected static-game controls.
After an ordinary restart, verify persistence and ask the owner for the same
short game/load/save check. Compare mutable saves/databases separately.

Second target: continue only after the first checkpoint passes, down to at least
64 GiB available, with additional metadata/next-write headroom. Repeat full
generated-payload verification, controls, ordinary restart and short game check.
Filler amount depends on actual free space; no fixed empty-volume size is armed.

A 32 GiB reserve or smaller is not released by this plan. Review it separately
after the 64 GiB checkpoint if the evidence justifies further proximity testing.
No forced power-loss test, deliberate corruption or repair of populated drives
is part of these stages. No new firmware is presently required.

## Physical coverage and claim limits

After the native checkpoints, use a read-only PC inspection to map representative
file extents and prove actual high-device coverage. Native byte hashes alone do
not establish physical placement. Keep source/static-game baselines and expected
mutable-data differences distinct. Sparse logical size is not allocated capacity.

Successful checkpoints qualify only the tested console, firmware, drive, occupied
capacity and ordinary operations. They do not prove universally data-loss-free
support, sudden-power-loss recovery, all repair paths or greater-than-2-TB support.
Greater than 2 TB remains a later phase. Do not promise a fixed number of
roundtrips or a completion duration before actual transfer/native results.
